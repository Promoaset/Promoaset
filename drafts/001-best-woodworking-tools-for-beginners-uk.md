Starting woodworking does not require a fully equipped workshop. A small set of dependable tools is usually more useful than a large collection bought before you know which projects you enjoy. The right first purchases depend on whether you plan to make shelves, repair furniture, build small boxes or work with reclaimed timber.

This guide is a buying framework rather than a list of products. Product prices, specifications and stock change, so check the manufacturer and retailer pages before buying.

## The sensible first set

### 1. A measuring and marking kit

Start with a tape measure, a combination square and a sharp pencil or marking knife. Accurate marking prevents many beginner errors before a cut is made. Choose tools that are easy to read and comfortable to hold rather than buying the cheapest set available.

### 2. A handsaw suited to your first projects

A general-purpose panel saw can handle simple crosscuts, but a fine-toothed backsaw may be easier for smaller, controlled work. If you are considering a powered saw, read the manufacturer’s instructions and make sure the tool suits your workspace, power supply and dust-control setup.

### 3. A block plane or sanding setup

A small block plane can be useful for trimming edges, but it takes practice to sharpen and set up. Sandpaper is less expensive to start with and works for many small projects. Buy a range of grits rather than a single very coarse sheet; use the least aggressive abrasive that achieves the result you need.

### 4. Clamps

Two or four clamps make assembly easier and help hold work safely while glue cures. Match the clamp type and throat depth to the projects you expect to make. Check the clamping capacity before buying, and protect finished surfaces with suitable pads or scrap material.

### 5. A safe work surface

A stable bench, workmate or other secure support is as important as the cutting tool. The work must not move unexpectedly. Keep the cutting path clear, support long stock and follow the instructions supplied with every tool.

## Hand tools or power tools first?

Hand tools are quieter and can suit a small space, but they require technique and sometimes sharpening. Power tools can save time, but they bring additional noise, dust and entanglement hazards. For a first project, choose the approach that you can use safely, store safely and maintain properly.

## What I would choose first

For a beginner making small shelves or boxes, I would start with measuring tools, a handsaw, clamps, sanding supplies and a stable work surface. Add a drill only when a project genuinely needs it. Delay specialist tools until a repeated task shows that the tool would save time or improve accuracy.

## Safety note

Read the manufacturer’s instructions before using any tool. Secure the work, keep hands away from cutting edges, use suitable eye and hearing protection where required, and control dust and ventilation for the material and process. Do not rely on this article as a substitute for the manufacturer’s instructions or task-specific safety guidance.
